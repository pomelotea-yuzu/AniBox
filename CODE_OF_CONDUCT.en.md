# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

> **Language:** English | [简体中文](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md)

## Our pledge

In the interest of fostering an open and welcoming environment, we as contributors and maintainers pledge
to make participation in this project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression,
level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, religion,
or sexual identity and orientation.

## Our standards

Examples of behaviour that creates a positive environment:

- Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
- Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints and experiences
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
- Accepting responsibility, apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from them
- Focusing on what is best for the community, not just for ourselves

Examples of unacceptable behaviour:

- The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without explicit permission
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## Responsibilities and scope

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards and will take appropriate and
fair corrective action in response to behaviour they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful.

This code applies in all project spaces (issues, PRs, discussions, code review) and when an individual is
representing the project in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Maintenance responsibility sits with GitHub `pomelotea-yuzu` / Gitee `yuzu-tea-duck`. Complaints may check
whether direct messages are available on the [Gitee maintainer profile](https://gitee.com/yuzu-tea-duck);
channel availability must be verified by the reporter, and an account name is not a universal DM address.
If contact is difficult, publicly ask for a private contact method without personal details or complaint
content, or use the platform's own reporting feature. Technical security issues follow the
[security policy](SECURITY.en.md) instead.

Reports should include where and when it happened, what happened, and necessary evidence. Reporters are
not required to identify themselves publicly or confront the person involved. Provide only the necessary
fragments, with API keys, unrelated identities and business content redacted; do not publish full chat
logs or an entire data directory as evidence. Maintainers store and share information on a need-to-know
basis, do not expose a reporter's identity in public statements without permission, and will not retaliate
against good-faith reports.

Handling starts with establishing the facts and giving the people involved a chance to respond, then
applies measures proportional to severity and repetition — asking for correction, a warning, removing
content, restricting or banning participation. In urgent cases harmful content may be limited first and
reviewed afterwards. Decisions record the reasoning, scope and, where applicable, the duration.

Those involved may add facts and request reconsideration. **There is currently a single maintainer and no
independent appeal body.** If a complaint concerns the maintainer, that conflict of interest will be
disclosed, a suitable non-conflicted person will be invited with consent where available, and if a fair
process is impossible that limitation will be stated explicitly; platform-level behaviour can be escalated
to the platform's own reporting channel.
